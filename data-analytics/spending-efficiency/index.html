Spent more, or spent better?

Manchester City have been found guilty on 114 of the Premier League's 115 charges. Did City win simply because their owner let them outspend everyone else? Every top-six club spent heavily. This page asks which of them got the most points for the money, and whether the costs City hid change the answer.

What the wage bill buys

Each dot is one club in one season. Across: its wage bill against the top-six average that season (1.0× = average), so 2010 and 2024 pounds compare fairly. Up: points. The line is what that level of spending earns on average. Dots above the line got more than their money's worth.

Points above expected, per season

Average gap between actual points and the line, with a 95% confidence interval (bootstrap). An interval that clears zero is unlikely to be luck.

What a point cost

Wages plus net transfer spend, divided by points won. Cheaper isn't the whole story: the last 10 points of a title race cost far more than the first 60.

Sources: results from openfootball (public domain); transfer fees from Transfermarkt via the open ewenme/transfers and dcaribou/transfermarkt-datasets extracts; wage bills and revenue from each club's published accounts (one source per figure in the repo); EUR to GBP at July–June average daily rates (Federal Reserve H.10); verdict figures from the Independent Commission's redacted decision, as analysed by The Esk and reported by Sky Sports and others.
Limits: correlation, not causation: managers, injuries and luck all move points. Wages are whole-club staff costs as published. Hidden costs are spread evenly across 2009-10 to 2017-18 because the decision does not give the timing of every item. City are appealing; no sanctions had been set when this was built.
