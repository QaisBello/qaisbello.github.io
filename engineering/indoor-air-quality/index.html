BreathingRoom

Five days of minute-by-minute air readings inside a 206-seat lecture theatre at the University of Lagos. When a lecture fills the room, CO2 climbs past 3,000 ppm. Each student is getting about 2 litres of fresh air a second.

The verdict
  • !Under-ventilated when full. CO2 stays above 1,000 ppm for three-quarters of the monitored minutes.
  • ~Dust comes in from outside. Indoor PM2.5 tracks the harmattan haze outdoors, at about three-quarters of its level.
  • ✓Formaldehyde is fine. No 30-minute average came near the WHO guideline.

03 · Ventilation, measured from CO2

How fast does the room clear?

When people leave, CO2 falls back toward the outdoor level exponentially. The rate of that fall is the air-change rate, measured here as roughly 1.2 air changes an hour. Four sessions had a clean enough fall to fit.

Fit: C(t) − Cout = (C0 − Cout)·e−ACH·t, starting 5 minutes after the peak. If people stayed in the room, the true rate is higher, so treat these as lower bounds.

What would fix it

Fresh air per person

At a CO2 peak the level is momentarily steady. At that point, CO2 above outdoors equals what each person breathes out divided by the fresh air they receive. Drag to see what more air would do.

The first version used a hand-built workbook. This one starts again from the monitors' own log files.

Data: field study by students of the Department of Building, University of Lagos: three BLATN indoor monitors, an INKBIRD outdoor monitor, a CO2/temperature logger and an 89-response occupant survey. The survey file is anonymised; no names, emails or locations are published.
Reference values: WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines 2021 (PM2.5 15 µg/m³, PM10 45 µg/m³, 24-hour). WHO Indoor Air Quality Guidelines 2010 (formaldehyde 0.1 mg/m³, 30-minute). These 24-hour guidelines are compared with 3-hour session means, so treat the comparison as indicative. CO2 generation of 0.0046 L/s per person for seated young adults follows Persily & de Jonge (2017). ASHRAE does not set a CO2 limit; 1,000 ppm is shown only as a common reference line.
