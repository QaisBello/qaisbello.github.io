Experiment readout · e-commerce landing page · Jan 2017

Ship the new page? No.

The redesigned landing page was tested against the current one on 290,584 users. It didn't convert better, and the test was large enough to say so with confidence.

02 · Stability

The estimate settled as data came in

Cumulative difference (new − old) with its 95% interval, day by day. The interval narrows and stays centred just below zero, so stopping after three weeks didn't cut the test short.

03 · Chance alone

What "no difference" looks like

10,000 simulated tests where both pages convert at the same rate. The observed result sits well inside that spread: 90% of no-effect runs did at least as well for the new page.

04 · Segments

No country breaks the pattern

Difference in conversion by country, with 95% intervals. Every interval crosses zero; the interaction terms in the regression are not significant either.

05 · Regression

Logistic regression check

converted ~ new page × country (US baseline). Odds ratios near 1 and large p-values: neither the page nor the country moves conversion.

07 · Data checks

What was cleaned before testing

Data: a public A/B test dataset of an e-commerce landing page redesign (Udacity Data Analyst programme), 294,478 page-view rows from January 2017. Analysis: two-proportion z-test, simulation under the null, logistic regression and power analysis in Python (pandas, SciPy, statsmodels). By Qais Adeniji-Bello.
